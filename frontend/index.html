Methodology & about

How it works, step by step

A second-year B.Tech AIML internship project that connects a scikit-learn model and a rule-based engine to this website through a FastAPI backend.

1

Dataset

the records with 17 lifestyle features and a 1-5 rating.

2

Target & split

Rating becomes Low / Medium / High. One stratified 80:20 split.

3

Preprocessing

Impute, scale numbers, one-hot encode categories, fitted on training data only.

4

Compare models

Baseline, Logistic Regression, Decision Tree and Random Forest with 5-fold CV.

5

Tune

GridSearchCV on the strongest model, using training data only.

6

Evaluate once

Scored on the untouched test set (20% of the rows).

7

Refit & save

The pipeline is refitted on all rows and saved for the API.

8

FastAPI

Endpoints for prediction, analytics and model insights.

9

Rule engine

12 hand-written rules turn your answers into prioritised suggestions. Separate from the model.

10

This website

Shows real results from the endpoints. No sample numbers.

Dataset

Each record has 17 lifestyle answers plus a 1-5 rating. ParticipantID and Gender are not model inputs. The text "None" in community involvement and physical activity is a real category. How the data was collected is unknown, so results are described as patterns in this sample.

Preprocessing

  • 5 numeric features: median imputation and standard scaling.
  • 12 categorical features: most-frequent imputation and one-hot encoding.
  • Everything is inside one scikit-learn Pipeline fitted on training data only, so no test information leaks in.

Machine-learning algorithms

  • A dummy baseline, Logistic Regression, Decision Tree and Random Forest are compared.
  • Models are ranked by macro F1 because the classes are imbalanced.
  • A separate Linear Regression experiment predicts the numeric rating.

Recommendation engine

Twelve rules cover transport, energy, waste, plastic, electricity, water, diet, local food, clothing, brands, community and awareness. Each suggestion has a priority and shows the answer that triggered it. Habits you already follow earn positive feedback, and electricity and water are compared with this dataset's median and 75th percentile, not with universal standards.

Honest evaluation

  • Class probabilities are uncalibrated vote shares.
  • Some profiles repeat in the data, which can make scores look better than for new people.
  • The notebook also reports a group-aware cross-validation check.

Technology

Python, pandas and scikit-learn for the model; FastAPI and Pydantic for the backend; plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript for this website, with charts drawn as SVG by the page itself.

What this project is not. It does not calculate carbon emissions or any real environmental impact. The rating is a label from the dataset, associations in the data are not causes, and suggestions are general ideas that do not guarantee a higher rating or a measurable improvement.
